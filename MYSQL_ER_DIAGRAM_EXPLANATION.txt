# Career Portal: MySQL Relational Schema Explanation

This document provides a comprehensive evaluation and explanation of the MySQL database architecture used in the Career Portal.

## 1. Core Entities & Relationships

### A. STUDENTS (Primary Hub)
- **Role**: Central table for all student interactions. It stores both profile summaries (domain, test status) and counters (bookReadCount, meetingCount) for fast frontend display.
- **Sync Note**: This table is synchronized with MongoDB via the unique `email` field.

### B. TEST_RESULTS (Analytics)
- **Relationship**: 1:N (One Student to many Test Results).
- **Function**: Stores granular scores for the career assessment (Systems, Enterprise, Automation, Intelligence). This data powers the "Recommended Path" logic.

### C. EDUCATION_DETAILS (Profile)
- **Relationship**: 1:1 or 1:N (depending on student history).
- **Function**: Captures degree, graduation year, and college details to help mentors understand the student's background.

### D. ONE_ON_ONE_SCHEDULES (Mentorship)
- **Relationship**: 1:N (One Student to many Meeting Requests).
- **Function**: Tracks the lifecycle of mentorship meetings. Key fields include `status` (Requested/Booked/Rejected) and `meetLink` (provided by Google Calendar integration).

### E. STUDENT_BOOK_HISTORY & BOOKS (Library)
- **Relationship**: N:M (Many-to-Many).
- **Junction Table**: `student_book_history` maps which student has read which book, including the `readAt` timestamp.
- **Function**: Powers the "Library" and "Course Materials" section of the portal.

### F. ADMINS & STUDENT_FEEDBACK
- **Admins**: Manages credentials and roles for staff accessing the CRM.
- **Feedback**: Stores ratings (1-5) and testimonials, linked back to the student who provided them.

## 2. Technical Implementation
- **Driver**: `mysql2/promise` with connection pooling for high concurrency.
- **Migration**: Managed via scripts in the `/database` directory (e.g., `migrate_final.cjs`).
- **Optimization**: Indexes are placed on `email` and `student_id` to ensure sub-millisecond query times even with thousands of records.
